Concept lookbook · fictional destination

Kazan-yu 火山湯

A spa built inside a sleeping volcano. Hot spring water rises at the crater and you follow it down, pool by pool, into the mountain.

The organising idea

One stream, followed downhill

Everything at Kazan-yu is fed by a single spring, and the spa is laid out along its path. The water is hottest at the top, around 44 °C, and it loses heat on the way down through each pool, corridor and cavern. A guest's visit is a slow descent: you start under the open sky and finish deep underground beside the river.

  • Gravity-fed throughout. No visible pumps in guest areas. Water reaches every pool, tap and table aqueduct by falling, so you can always hear it moving.
  • Temperature as wayfinding. Each level is a few degrees cooler than the one above. The floor stones are warmer near hotter zones, so bare feet can tell you where you are.
  • Yukata colour by depth. Guests change robes as they descend: undyed linen at the crater, indigo in the caverns, near-black at the river.
  • The heartbeat. Low-frequency microphones in the magma chamber play the mountain's own rumble, slowed down, in the deepest rooms.

Every floor on one grid

Level 0 · Arrival

The Basalt Gate

Guests walk in through a torii cut from a single basalt column. Inside, one wall of the arrival hall is thick glass with real lava moving slowly behind it. Shoes come off here and you don't put them back on until you leave.

Level B1 · Every connecting route

Kiri-no-Michi, the mist corridors

The cave has no dry hallways. Each connecting passage is a shallow reflecting pool, ankle-deep and warm, with a knee-high layer of mist rolling across it. You cross barefoot on stepping stones set just under the surface.

  • Mist that stays low. Cool air is drawn in at floor level over the warm water, so the fog forms close to the surface and hangs there. Ultrasonic foggers in the walls fill gaps on quieter days.
  • Stones you can feel. Stepping stones are polished smooth on top and textured at the edges, so you can follow the path by touch when the mist is thick.
  • Lantern niches at ankle height. Light comes from low in the walls so it lights the mist from inside, and each lantern is doubled in the water.
  • Quiet hours. After 22:00 the corridors are silent. A small bell at each junction is the only sound allowed.
  • Koi in the wider pools. At junctions the corridor widens into small ponds with black and gold koi moving under the mist.

Level B2 · Over the underground river

Dining

Kawagiri 川霧, the river-mist restaurant

A timber dining deck hung on heavy beams over a fast underground river. Every table sits on its own platform behind a falling curtain of water. Soy sauce and hot tea reach each table through tiny wooden aqueducts. The building is extravagant; the food is simple. Ramen, smoke, neon and steam.

  • The aqueducts. Header tanks above the kitchen feed hinoki channels on thin stilts. At your table you lift a small wooden sluice gate and soy sauce trickles into your dish; a second channel brings tea. A third channel for dashi or warm sake could run to the counter.
  • Curtains that part. The water curtain stops for a few seconds when a server steps up to the table, then starts again, so every course arrives like a reveal.
  • Arriving by boat. Guests come in on a lantern-lit flat-bottomed boat along the river and climb up a ladder-stair to the deck.
  • Neon, kept small. Only the restaurant uses magenta and cyan neon, in hand-bent kanji signs. Everywhere else the light is lantern amber, so the neon reads as a destination.
  • Cooked by the volcano. Eggs are lowered into the spring in baskets, and dumplings and vegetables steam over a fumarole vent in the kitchen.

Level B3 · The great cavern

Aqua Cavern: ten waters

The spa's water park. The same spring runs through all ten attractions from top to bottom, getting cooler and calmer as it goes, until it reaches the sea in the wave grotto.

01

Caldera Crown Infinity Pool

42 °C

Carved into the crater rim, where the spring first surfaces. The edge spills over into a sea of cloud. Opens at dawn so guests can watch the sun come up over the clouds.

02

Lava Tube Lazy River

38 °C

A 600-metre float through an ancient lava tube on woven rings. The ceiling has amber light in its cracks and glowworms between them.

03

Magma Glass Slide

36 °C

A clear tube slide that spirals down past the lava window, close enough that the water inside glows orange.

04

Geyser Plaza

37 °C

Floor vents fire in timed sequence around a ring of stone seats. The largest goes off every 20 minutes, and guests gather to watch it.

05

Obsidian Mirror Float Pool

35 °C

Dense mineral salt water, so you float without effort. It's perfectly still and black, so the glowworm ceiling reflects in it and you seem to hang in a starfield.

06

Fumarole Steam Grotto

45–65 °C

A chain of natural steam rooms getting hotter as you go in, from 45 to 55 to 65 °C. Hinoki ladles and buckets for cooling off.

07

Glacier Plunge Ice Cave

6 °C

Blue ice where it meets the basalt, a cold plunge fed by meltwater. One door leads straight back into the steam.

08

Rimstone Cascade Terraces

40 → 33 °C

Dozens of scalloped mineral pools, each spilling into the one below. Choose your temperature by choosing your step.

09

Thunder Curtain Falls

39 °C

A heavy hot waterfall with stone seats set under focused streams for a shoulder massage. A walkway runs behind the falling water.

10

Wave Grotto

30 °C

Where the spring finally meets the sea. Slow heated waves roll onto a black sand beach inside a sea cave, with the open ocean beyond.

Carved into the north wall

Suites and identity

Each suite gets its own trickle from the stream above, filling a private stone tub on a ledge over the terraces. The brand stays quiet: materials first, logo second.

More ideas for the pile

Extras

  • Onsen sommelier. A host who reads your mood and builds a route through the pools by mineral content and temperature.
  • Ash mud bath. Warm volcanic clay pits in a side chamber, rinsed off under a bamboo shower.
  • Tamago lowering. Guests tie their own egg basket to a rope at the spring source, and get it back for breakfast the next day.
  • Tsukimi deck. A moon-viewing platform on the crater rim with sake served warm from the spring.
  • Lantern release on the river. Floating paper lanterns sent downstream from Kawagiri, collected at the wave grotto.
  • Geothermal everything. The volcano powers the whole resort. A brass gauge in the lobby shows how much it's producing right now.
  • Basalt key token. A small polished stone opens your locker and suite. Guests keep it when they leave.
